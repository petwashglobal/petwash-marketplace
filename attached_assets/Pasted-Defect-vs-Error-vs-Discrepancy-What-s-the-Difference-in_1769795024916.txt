In software testing, we often hear terms like defect, error, and discrepancy used as if they’re interchangeable. But they’re not. Understanding the nuances between them can help testers, developers, and stakeholders communicate more clearly and catch issues earlier in the software development lifecycle.
Here’s a quick breakdown of what each term means — and why the differences matter.
🔹 What is an Error?
An error is a human mistake.
It usually occurs during development, design, or requirement specification. An error leads to incorrect logic, calculations, or behavior in the application.
🧠 Example: A developer mistakenly writes if (x > 10) instead of if (x >= 10), introducing a subtle logic error.
❗ Errors are the root cause of most defects.
🔹 What is a Defect?
A defect is a problem in the product — a fault in the code, logic, design, or requirements that causes it to behave incorrectly or not as expected.
🔍 Example: The incorrect condition written above (due to the error) causes the app to block eligible users from accessing a feature. That’s a defect.
❗ Defects are what we test to find.
🔹 What is a Discrepancy?
A discrepancy is a mismatch between the actual behavior and the expected behavior, whether it’s in the UI, documentation, test case, or specification.

🎨 Example: The product displays a blue “Submit” button, but the design spec clearly states it should be green. Functionally, it works — but visually, it’s a discrepancy.
❗ Not all discrepancies are defects — but they still need resolution.
🧠 Why This Matters in Real Life
Developers need to trace back defects to their root errors.
Testers need to log discrepancies clearly — not everything is a “bug.”
Product teams need to differentiate between cosmetic issues and functional failures.
Precise terminology avoids miscommunication and unnecessary rework.

Final Thoughts
In a fast-paced Agile world, understanding the difference between error, defect, and discrepancy helps teams build better software — faster.
So next time you see something off in your app, ask yourself:
➡️ Is this an error someone made?
➡️ Is it a defect in the system?
➡️ Or is it a discrepancy in what’s expected?
Sometimes, the smallest distinctions make the biggest impact.
